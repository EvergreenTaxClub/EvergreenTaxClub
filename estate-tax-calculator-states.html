How to read this illustration

State tax uses one estate at the survivor’s projected death. All property is assumed to be taxable in the selected state; the starting estate is treated as net of ordinary deductions before death taxes. No marital/charitable deductions, prior gifts, state DSUE, out-of-state apportionment, business/farm relief, inheritance-tax offsets, or other special credits are modeled. This is a planning estimate, not a completed tax return.

For future years, the chosen inflation rate changes the state exemption only. Rates, tax caps, and bracket widths are held constant. For credit-based Massachusetts and Rhode Island, the credit is modeled to match the projected exemption; New York retains its exclusion phaseout. D.C. retains nominal bracket thresholds. Custom inflation represents hypothetical law changes for fixed-exemption states. Washington uses the schedule effective July 1, 2026, including for the year-end 2026 starting point. The deduction for state estate taxes follows IRS Form 706 guidance.

State projections use the published 2026 exemption × (1 + assumed growth)^(endpoint year − 2026), rounded to whole dollars. The base date stays 2026 even if you change the estate starting year. Fixed thresholds stay constant in current-policy mode; indexed thresholds use the editable inflation assumption. This is a scenario, not a prediction of CPI, statutory rounding, or future law. No estate tax is shown as “Not applicable,” never as a zero-dollar exemption.

Sources are embedded beside each state. The Tax Foundation October 2026 survey supports the nationwide tax coverage and indexing classification; state agency sources supply the estate-tax thresholds. Snapshot is not automatically updated when this file is reopened.

This model follows the supplied estate-tax reports: compound the estate annually, round the endpoint estate to whole dollars, grow each spouse’s exclusion with assumed inflation, round each exclusion down to the nearest $10,000, and combine the two. Tax amounts are rounded to whole dollars. State estate tax is computed from the selected state schedule. Federal tax = selected federal rate × max(0, estate − estimated state estate tax − combined federal exclusion). This assumes the state estate tax qualifies for the federal deduction. Total tax is the sum of the two, not their combined headline rates. Inheritance tax is excluded.

Spousal exclusion limitation: growing both exclusions through the endpoint is a simplified comparison assumption. Actual portability depends on the first death, unused exclusion, and a valid election; a deceased spouse’s unused exclusion does not keep receiving inflation adjustments. Prior gifts, spending, contributions, fees, investment taxes, deductions, and estate-planning structures are not modeled.

The horizon is user-selected, not a life-expectancy estimate. Returns, inflation, and future law can differ. The default $15 million per-spouse exclusion is for 2026; changing the start year does not automatically update tax assumptions. Use this as an illustration, not a tax return or individualized tax determination.

Reference: IRS Form 706 instructions. No internet connection is needed to run the calculator; the reference link opens online.
