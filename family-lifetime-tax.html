How the projection works & sources

The 2026 MFJ ordinary brackets are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%, with upper thresholds of $24,800, $100,800, $211,400, $403,550, $512,450, and $768,700. Long-term gains stack above taxable ordinary income, with 0% through $98,900, 15% through $613,700, then 20%.

The standard deduction starts at $32,200 plus $1,650 for each spouse aged 65+. These thresholds and deductions grow at your chosen inflation rate without IRS rounding. The temporary senior deduction is included through 2028: up to $6,000 per eligible spouse, reduced by 6% of joint MAGI above $150,000 per spouse. Modeled gross income is used as MAGI.

NIIT is 3.8% of the smaller of gains or MAGI above $250,000 (not inflation indexed). This assumes the ordinary income field is not net investment income. The gains field is treated as realized capital gains for state calculations; do not combine qualified dividends with gains when projecting state tax. collectibles and real-estate recapture are not supported.

State-tax estimate

State schedules use the Tax Foundation 2026 MFJ compilation with selected official updates linked below. Some reference thresholds are carried over from 2025; this is a simplified planning model, not a complete current state return. Federal standard deductions remain unchanged by your state choice. State calculations use their own deduction/exemption assumptions. Most states tax gains as ordinary income; selected exclusions and surtaxes are modeled. Nonrefundable state credits are generally omitted.

Retirement-withdrawal exemptions are modeled for Illinois, Iowa, Mississippi, and Pennsylvania; limited retirement-withdrawal exclusions are included for Georgia and New York. Other retirement, Social Security, capital-gain, or income-specific relief is not automatic unless stated in the selected state's notes. The selected state's notes identify material omissions.

State rates are held constant after 2026, except Washington's enacted 2028 income tax. Scheduled future rate cuts are not forecast. State-threshold inflation is enabled by default and scales modeled dollar thresholds, deductions, and exclusions together. You can disable it. This is a scenario assumption, not state-specific statutory indexing. No local taxes, multistate sourcing, moving midyear, state tax credits, or federal itemized SALT deduction are included.

The projection begins in 2026 and includes the final year in full. Enter prior December 31 account balances, or use current balances as a planning approximation. Ordinary income is before standard deductions but after pre-tax payroll deductions; gains are realized profits, not sale proceeds. Income growth and investment returns compound annually. Federal brackets and deductions grow from the 2026 baseline using the inflation assumption, which also discounts taxes to 2026 dollars. Withdrawals use whole-year ages of 60 or later, beyond the usual age-59½ early-distribution threshold; the model assumes your plan permits the withdrawals. IRS distribution ages.

Each RMD uses the prior year-end balance divided by the IRS Uniform Lifetime factor. Starting ages follow birth cohort: 70½ before July 1949 (already past at this model's start), 72 for the remaining 1949–1950 cohorts, 73 for 1951–1959, and 75 for 1960 onward. Withdrawals occur at year-end after account growth, without first-year deferral. Optional withdrawals begin at each owner’s selected age (60 or later). The annual amount stays fixed in nominal dollars; enter $0 for RMD-only withdrawals. Actual withdrawals equal the greater of the elected amount and the RMD, capped at the balance after annual growth. Only actual withdrawals are taxed and deducted from balances. Excess withdrawals do not prepay future RMDs. No contributions, conversions, or still-working exception are modeled. All distributions are assumed fully taxable federally. Table II for a sole-beneficiary spouse more than 10 years younger is not modeled.

Income continues growing throughout the horizon; retirement does not automatically stop wages. Both spouses remain MFJ through the final year. This is a lifetime income-tax illustration, not an estate or survivor model: no estate tax, inherited-account withdrawals, local tax, payroll tax, AMT, credits, itemized deductions, QBI deduction, or automatic Social Security taxation. Remaining pre-tax assets may create taxes after this horizon. Future laws and actual returns can differ.
